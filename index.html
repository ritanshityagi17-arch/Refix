Circular Decision Matrix

The Three Pathways: Repair, Reuse, Replace

We prioritize longevity, circularity, and environmental stewardship, while balancing realistic economics and personal safety.

🔧

Repair

Fix It First • Restore Function

The highest priority in the circular economy. Restores original utility while keeping 95%+ of the existing material intact.

  • ✓ Repair cost < 50% of new replacement price
  • ✓ Spare parts or standard adhesives available
  • ✓ No structural hazard or toxic degradation
  • ✓ Maximizes carbon and resource preservation
🔄

Reuse & Repurpose

Give A Second Life • Upcycle

When primary function is impaired or uneconomical to restore, transform durable raw materials into creative, useful new items.

  • ✓ Primary repair cost > 65% of original value
  • ✓ Structural materials (wood, denim, metal) are intact
  • ✓ Old electronics can serve dedicated tasks (e.g. server)
  • ✓ Diverts 100% of the object from municipal landfills
♻️

Replace Responsibly

Certified Recycling • Safe Retirement

When an item compromises safety, contains toxic flaking coatings, or is irreparably broken. Always pair with certified recycling and durable replacements.

  • ✓ Critical safety risks (cracked helmet, peeling Teflon)
  • ✓ Repair costs exceed value with poor longevity
  • ✓ Divert via certified e-waste or metal scrap stations
  • ✓ Upgrade to modular, "Buy-It-For-Life" designs

Why REFIX?

Modern consumer culture pushes a default narrative: if it is damaged, throw it out and buy a new one. This fuels planned obsolescence, depletes virgin resources, and fills municipal landfills.

REFIX inverts this dynamic. By evaluating items across all material domains—not just electrical electronics—we democratize circular decision-making, celebrate the Right-to-Repair, and make sustainable choices economically obvious.

📐 Algorithmic Methodology & Benchmarks

1. Repairability Score Heuristic

Score = (MaterialEase × 0.30) + (SeverityScore × 0.35) + (PartsAvailability × 0.20) + (UserSkill × 0.15)
Scores above 55% indicate strong viability for home or community restoration.

2. Economic Viability Ratio (EVR)

EVR = RepairCost / ReplacementCost
• EVR ≤ 0.50: Favorable repair zone.
• 0.50 < EVR ≤ 0.70: Secondary consideration (depends on sentimental value and age).
• EVR > 0.70: Triggers REUSE or REPLACE pathway.

3. Safety & Toxicity Veto Rules

Certain materials trigger an immediate REPLACE veto regardless of cost: • Damaged PTFE / Teflon cookware (toxic fluoropolymer ingestion risk)
• Fractured impact helmet EPS foam (permanent structural shock loss)
• Swollen lithium-ion batteries (thermal runaway fire hazard)

4. Carbon & Water Savings Benchmarks

Derived from life-cycle analysis (LCA) data per kilogram of material avoided (e.g. 15 kg CO₂e/kg denim cotton, 45 kg CO₂e/kg modular electronics, 1.8 kg CO₂e/kg solid wood).
